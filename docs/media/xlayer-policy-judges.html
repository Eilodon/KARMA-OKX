Start here if you're not a blockchain person

Picture hiring a subcontractor you've never met and handing them your card with no limit. That's what an AI agent does every time it's authorized to pay another agent for a task. KARMA is the underwriter that sits in between: it reads the agent's track record, reads how risky the specific task is (moving custody funds is riskier than a routine rebalance), and compiles both into one enforceable set of terms — a per-transaction cap, a cumulative cap, a bond percentage, whether a human/panel evaluator must sign off. That policy isn't a suggestion in a dashboard. It's recorded on-chain, and the contract itself rejects any execution attempt that breaks it.

An underwriter, not a credit score

A number alone can't say how much an agent should be allowed to spend on this specific obligation. KARMA compiles principal cap ∩ earned trust ∩ task risk into one artifact.

The handful of technical words this page uses, in one sentence each
Policy
The compiled output: per-tx cap, cumulative cap, bond %, evaluator requirement, expiry — hashed into one policyHash.
Task risk
A classification of how risky the specific obligation is (e.g. "custody required, irreversible") — tightens terms independently of the agent's own history.
Bond
A percentage of the transaction the agent must post as collateral, scaled up for riskier tasks.
Evaluator
Who must sign off on a dispute for this policy: none, a single arbiter, or an N-of-M panel — set by risk tier.
EIP-712 signed issuance
A principal can authorize a policy by signature instead of paying gas directly — anyone can submit it on their behalf.
PolicyCredentialProof
A zero-knowledge proof that an agent clears a reputation threshold without revealing the exact score, bound to this specific policy request so it can't be replayed.
